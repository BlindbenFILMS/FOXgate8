## SOURCE 1 · Ora Clinical, "Endpoints and Technologies" · https://oraclinical.com/endpoints-and-technologies/ (read 14 Nov 2026)
- NAME: the section is headed "Ora-VNC™ mobility courses". VNC = Visual Navigation Course.
- WHO MADE IT + WHAT FOR: Ora designed it. It assesses mobility and functional vision in patients with inherited retinal diseases (IRD).
- THE ROOM: the patient is placed inside what the page calls a "360-degree visual challenge environment".
- WHAT IT MEASURES: a treatment's effect, through improvement in the patient's functional mobility.
- NO ONE COURSE: the page says "no single course is appropriate for all IRDs". It names three kinds of loss, each with one disease: CENTRAL vision loss (Leber's hereditary optic neuropathy, LHON) · LIGHT PERCEPTION loss (Leber's congenital amaurosis, LCA) · PERIPHERAL vision loss (retinitis pigmentosa, RP). Ora changes the course to suit the disease the treatment is for.
- DIFFICULTY: the courses can be set to different difficulty levels, for different patient groups and conditions.
- REGULATORS: Ora's mobility courses have been presented to the FDA as endpoints in gene therapy, oligonucleotide, RNA therapy and medical device programs. The page states the VNC "has been accepted by the FDA as a primary endpoint in the United States."

### The text
- NAME: "Ora-VNC™ Mobility Courses"; VNC written out here as Visual Navigation Challenge.
- WHY: trials in inherited retinal disease needed FUNCTIONAL endpoints. The courses were made to evaluate gene therapies for IRDs and are used as functional endpoints in clinical trials worldwide.
- WHAT A WALK SHOWS: an objective reading of a change in the patient's mobility that follows a gain in vision from treatment.
- ALSO (p 4): they pick up differences in visual function impairment specific to IRDs · they give a clinically meaningful reading through "a 360-degree visual challenge environment" · they challenge the particular parts of vision that IRDs wear down.
- SETUPS BY DISEASE + SEVERITY (p 4, the heading Ben pointed to): Ora offers several courses for different TYPES of vision loss and different SEVERITIES. The range runs from "mild visual field constriction (with preserved central acuity)" to "light perception" only.

- STANDARDIZATION (p 5): ambient light levels are standardized, "independent of room size and features" · there are several versions ("iterations") of each course, so a patient cannot learn one by heart · presented to the FDA as endpoints in gene therapy, oligonucleotide, RNA therapy and medical device programs.
- KEY FEATURES (p 5, our words): can assess a therapy's effect on IRDs · sensitive to change in visual function · built for multi-centre studies · difficulty levels for different patient groups · easy to use and highly mobile · comes with a training system and a standard protocol · a fully masked, independent reading center for objective scoring.

- AROUND IT (p 1, about Ora, not the course): more than 45 clinical trials in retinal indications in the last five years.

### SOURCE 1 · Endpoints and Technologies: the rest of the page
- WHY ORA BUILDS ENDPOINTS: Ora keeps its own research and development (R&D) team. The page says older endpoints and trial designs often lack precise control of confounders; the data is noisier; a study then needs a bigger sample, or is not sensitive to change early in a disease. R&D's three aims: measure a product's efficacy accurately · understand the therapy's full impact · streamline trial designs and sample needs. Its stated goal is to cut the noise in a sponsor's data: more statistical power, a better chance of success, a clearer therapy-placebo difference.
- THE OTHER ENDPOINTS + TOOLS ON THE PAGE (six, beside the course):
  - ORA CAE® (Controlled Adverse Environment): standardizes temperature, humidity, airflow, lighting and visual tasking for a constant, repeatable challenge; fewer patients, sites and less time. FDA-accepted for Phase 2 and 3 studies as a primary endpoint in the US.
  - IVAD (Inter-blink Interval Visual Acuity Decay): a measure of visual function, made by Ora R&D, to show a therapy's effect on ocular surface degradation. Validated by Ora as a secondary endpoint in the US, Europe and Latin America.
  - OPI 2.0© (Ocular Protection Index 2.0 System): measures tear film breakup, as a time (TFBUT) and as an area (mean surface breakup area), under normal visual conditions. The OPI is validated as a secondary endpoint in the US.
  - ORA EYECUP™ (powered by SDC Capture): captures patient data inside and outside the clinic; more data points in a shorter time; less variability.
  - LISSAMINE GREEN: a stain that shows the health of the conjunctiva; more consistent across visits than sodium fluorescein; validated by Ora as a secondary endpoint in the US, Europe and Latin America; R&D is making a new grading scale.
  - ORA-CAC® (Conjunctival Allergen Challenge Model): used for over 40 years to evaluate anti-allergic agents for allergic conjunctivitis; for most of those programs its studies were pivotal for FDA approval; validated severity scales accepted by regulators worldwide.
- SITE-WIDE (the menu on both pages): over 3,000 clinical ophthalmology projects completed · "over 50 years" of guidance · THE BLIND CANVAS PROJECT has its own page under WHY ORA: https://oraclinical.com/blind-canvas-project/ · contact: https://oraclinical.com/contact-us/ · headquarters 138 Haverhill Street, Suite 102, Andover, Massachusetts 01810, USA · tel +1 978 685 8900.
